## 5.2 Communication and capacity

- **WhatsApp:** agreed channel for short progress messages, availability and blockers.
- **Discord:** agreed channel for detailed discussions, screen sharing and scheduled meetings.
- **Daily discussions:** each member reports what changed, what comes next, dependencies and any blocked work. Summarise material decisions/actions in the relevant issue or controlled log.
- **Meetings every second day after 11:00:** inspect progress and linked artefacts, resolve policy/scope/requirement conflicts, plan reviews and record action owners. Confirm the exact start time for each meeting through the team channels.
- **Four hours per member every day:** confirmed by Ryno. Discussion, meetings, research, writing, review and corrections all count within this allowance, giving a gross planned capacity of 12 person-hours per day.

The team's WhatsApp/Discord use is for project coordination. It does not commit CivicConnect to integrating those platforms as product features.

## 5.3 Work and review cycle

1. Create/select an issue for each coherent work unit, with an owner, intended output, dependencies and completion criteria. Work backward from the M1 submission date of 9 September 2026 and leave time for both required reviews.
2. Work on a task branch from the latest controlled main state. Substantive direct development on main is not permitted [S4, section 9].
3. Verify sources, assumptions, identifiers, cross-links and acceptance implications before requesting review. Record material AI assistance and the human verification actually performed.
4. Open a PR linked to the issue and request both non-author members' reviews. Summarise the proposed change, rationale, affected artefacts and unresolved questions.
5. Reviewers inspect substance, including correctness, scope/requirement alignment, security/privacy, traceability and downstream verification implications. Give specific findings where correction is needed.
6. The author addresses comments, reviewers inspect corrections, and the final change receives both required independent approvals before merge. Self-approval and unexamined approval are not accepted [S4, sections 9-9.1].
7. Update related artefacts and the issue after the controlled change. Preserve the review and version evidence.

A drafted file is ready for review when it is coherent and source-supported with open assumptions visible. It is ready for baseline only when relevant questions are resolved, both reviews are complete and formal sign-off requirements are met.

## 5.4 Meetings, decisions and conflict resolution

Keep a short record for each actual meeting: date, attendees, artefacts examined, decisions, open questions and actions with owners/target dates. Link to issues or decision entries where practical. Do not record meetings that have not occurred.

For disagreements, compare the options against stakeholder value, required scope, evidence, capacity, quality, security and risk. Seek a reasoned agreement; record genuine significant decisions with rationale and consequences in Willem's Decision Log. If evidence is insufficient, record a justified deferment and the information needed. Ask the lecturer to resolve unclear assessment requirements; do not present internal assumptions as lecturer approval [S4, sections 12-14].

## 5.5 Delays and changes in availability

Report a likely missed task or review commitment promptly, identify completed work and explain the remaining dependency. Agree and record a revised handoff or redistribution. Track significant schedule variance and its engineering cause. Do not silently cut required security, testing or peer review to recover time [S4, section 18].

Each member should retain enough familiarity with the other artefacts to help resolve blockers and answer assessor questions. Reassignment does not justify invented contribution or attendance evidence.

## 5.6 AI, references and sensitive information

Each author remains accountable for material AI-assisted work. Record date, student, tool, engineering task, contribution, verification, decision and issues found in the team AI Usage Register. Verify important claims against credible sources or appropriate tests and be able to explain and modify the submitted work [S4, sections 10-10.1].

Use in-text citations and a reference list where claims depend on supplied or external material. Do not commit credentials or expose confidential/sensitive information to external AI systems. Actual repository configuration claims require project evidence, not a citation to this agreement [S4, sections 9-10; S1, section 9.1].
